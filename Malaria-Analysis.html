Kakamega CCMm: from a monthly export to a supervision shortlist

A single, reproducible notebook that turns the county's community case-management malaria (CCMm) spreadsheet into headline indicators, population-adjusted incidence, age-disaggregated rates, and a ranked list of exactly which community health units to visit next, the analysis behind the charts on the malaria project.

What the notebook does

Run top-to-bottom, it ingests one Excel export and emits a full evidence pack: a console summary, a summary.json, thirteen charts, and three supervision workbooks, no command line, no manual steps.

01Headline rates: testing rate, positivity (TPR) & treatment coverage
02Monthly trend and sub-county breakdown
03Data-quality, discrepancy and zero/missing checks
04Population-adjusted incidence per 1,000 / year
05Age-disaggregated testing & positivity (under-5 vs over-5)
06Ward/CHU hotspots, case concentration & supervision shortlist
01 · Reproducible by design

One file, run top to bottom, nothing to wire up

There's no main() and no command line, so nothing clashes with Jupyter. A rendering backend is set for headless machines, so the same notebook runs identically on a laptop or a server. Only one line, the input filename, ever needs changing.

02 · Trust the data first

      
Fail loudly on bad columns; coerce blanks to zero

      
Field exports are messy. The loader validates every expected column up front and raises a clear, human-readable error if the sheet has drifted, rather than silently producing wrong numbers. Count columns are coerced to numeric with blanks treated as zero, while a separate untouched copy is kept so the data-quality checks can see the data exactly as entered.

03 · Headline indicators

      
Three rates, computed once and reused everywhere

      
Testing rate, test positivity (TPR) and treatment coverage are derived through a single safe-division helper, so a month with zero suspected cases yields 0%, never a crash. The same figures feed the console summary, the JSON, and every chart, keeping the whole report internally consistent.

04 · Where the burden sits

      
Sub-county ranking against the county average

      
Ranking positivity by sub-county turns one aggregate number into a map of where to act. Matungu and Khwisero run well above the 58% county average, while Likuyani sits far below, a difference that changes where commodities and supervision should go.

      
    


    

      
05 · Population-adjusted incidence

      
Rates per 1,000, so places are compared fairly

      
Raw case counts favour big populations. Joining the 2019 census population to each sub-county and annualising from however many months are present converts counts into incidence and screening reach per 1,000 per year, the fair basis for comparing a large sub-county with a small one.

06 · From analysis to action

      
A named list of where to supervise next

      
The payoff isn't a chart. It's a decision. The notebook writes three monthly workbooks: the highest-burden CHUs to support, an operational-gaps shortlist, and the chronically silent units. Those silent CHUs report zero activity every month, so they never trip a threshold, which is exactly why they need a dedicated named list to visit, onboard, or retire from the register.
