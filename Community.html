Where the work started.

Before I designed MEAL systems, I organised people. The habits I rely on now, meeting communities where they already are, listening before measuring, and making every gathering count, were learned in Nyakach with no budget and no framework document.

Youth Mobilisation · Sport as Platform

The December 2015 holiday tournaments

As Vice Chairperson under the outgoing leadership, I used the extended December holiday window to run a major youth mobilisation strategy. Sport was the instrument. I had seen that a tournament could pull together students from different university chapters and village wards in a way that no meeting ever would.

It was open to every young person in Nyakach, not only those home from university and secondary school. Idle youth were increasingly seen locally as a security risk, and the fastest way to change that was to give them somewhere to be.

A sporting carnival across the sub-county

Football for boys and girls was hosted at centralised community fields at Urudi Primary and Urudi Secondary Schools, pulling village teams together to combat holiday idleness and surface raw local talent. Netball for girls and volleyball for boys ran in parallel, turning the fixture list into an inclusive, sub-county-wide carnival that brought hundreds of local youth, elders and professionals into the same space.

The captive-audience strategy

The crowds were the point, not the by-product

The tournaments were never only recreation. I used the crowds they drew as a platform for public youth advocacy, for talking openly about academic mentorship, and for laying the conceptual groundwork for the environmental drives that followed. An audience of hundreds, already gathered and already listening, is the most effective mobilisation channel a community organiser has.

Two players scouted into the junior leagues

The carnival drew scouts as well as crowds, and two of our footballers were signed into Kenyan junior leagues on the strength of what they played that December. The talent was always there. What Nyakach lacked was a stage where anyone could see it.

Prizes that stayed in the community

Winning teams in every category took home monetary awards, uniforms and balls, so the investment stayed with the teams and kept the sport alive long after the holiday ended.

Academic Mentorship · Self-Funded Delivery

The 2016 institutional school visitations

After the public success of the 2015 tournaments the transition took place and I took office as NUSA Chairperson. My administration immediately leveraged that momentum into a relentless academic mentorship and motivation tour.

The 15-school sprint

Using our own pooled resources and pocket money, my cabinet and I physically visited 15 secondary schools across Nyakach in the space of a single month, from a total of 54 in the sub-county. The following term the team returned to the road and covered another 20.

Impact and mindset shift

Proof that the community could fund its own development

The team gave intensive talks to thousands of high school students: career path guidance, university application insights, and plain exposure to what was possible. The tour is still remembered locally for shifting how students saw their own prospects, and for proving that NUSA could execute large-scale community development on its own resources, without waiting on political handouts.

Climate Resilience · Community Crowdfunding

Launching the Nyakach Go Green Initiative

The institutional trust built through the sports tournaments and the school mentorship tours gave my administration exactly the leverage needed to launch the Nyakach Go Green Initiative. Two years of showing up had earned the standing to ask for something bigger.

The M-Changa crowdfunding blueprint

A funding route the community owned outright

To guarantee full self-reliance and transparent community ownership, the 2016 cabinet established the Nyakach Go Green M-Changa fundraiser, an idea I originated. It let Nyakach professionals, alumni and the diaspora fund climate resilience efforts directly, with the flow of money visible to everyone contributing.

From sports fields to tree nurseries

The same youth networks that had competed in the 2015 tournaments were remobilised through the 2016 rainy seasons. NUSA members set up physical tree nurseries and planted in the very secondary schools they had visited for academic mentorship, all 54 of them, along with selected primaries. Siting the planting inside schools meant the environmental clubs already there would nurture the trees after we left, which is the only reason seedlings survive.

The initiative has run every year since its inception. That is the part I am proudest of, because a campaign that outlives the cabinet which started it was never really about the cabinet.
